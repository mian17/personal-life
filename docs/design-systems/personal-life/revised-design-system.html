Revised design system

The visual and behavioural contract behind the five revised app prototypes: Notes, Tasks, Meals, Focus and Feedback. One calm palette, one accent per app, a light and a dark scheme that share a vocabulary, and an 8pt grid throughout. Everything below is measured from the tokens the app files actually ship.

Five standalone apps Two schemes, one phrase No dependency, no network, no font file

1 · Principles

Five rules that decide every later question. When a component is ambiguous, one of these settles it.

  • One screen, one job. A screen shows one thing to do. Sub-screens replace the parent; they never stack on top of it.
  • Words carry state. Colour supports a status, it never is the status. Every chip, pill and bar has its word beside it.
  • Furniture stays quiet. Hairlines, not heavy dividers. One accent per app, used for selection and progress. No gradients, no glow.
  • Nothing moves unless the OS allows it. Every transition sits inside prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference. No loop, no autoplay, no ticker.
  • Fits a hand, at 414 wide. One column, 48px targets, safe-area padding. Nothing important lives within 20px of the bottom edge.

2 · Colour

Warm off-white --page in light, soft charcoal in dark — one ground per scheme, painted by the screen itself. Groups and cards sit above it on --surface, so the ground shows through the gaps between them. Neither scheme uses pure black or pure white for a large fill.

One accent per app carries selection, primary fill and progress; status colours stay muted and always pair with a word.

Colour is never the only signal

Every status appears as a pill with its own word — Overdue, Due today, Done, Won't do. The pill's tint is reinforcement. Remove all colour and the screen still reads correctly.

Motion is opt-in, per the OS

Every transition lives inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference). There is no animation that repeats, no auto-advancing carousel, no parallax, and nothing strobes. With reduced motion on, the interface has no travel at all — state is simply there.

6 · Layout

A single centred column, 414 CSS px wide — the iPhone 11 width the prototypes are drawn at — with viewport-fit=cover and safe-area padding so nothing hides under a notch or the home indicator. The frame paints the off-white ground edge to edge.

8 · Patterns

Six arrangements cover every screen in the five apps. New screens should be one of these, not a new shape.

List → detail, never a stack

Tapping a row replaces the screen. The back link sits top-left and names its parent ("Notes", "Meals"), so the header answers "where am I" without a title bar. Escape closes a sub-screen exactly as the back link does.

Toolbar save, no bottom button row

A create or edit screen puts its commit action in the header — a checkmark on the right — and its cancel on the left. The body is the form. There is no Save/Cancel pair at the foot of an editor.

Settings: Appearance first, feedback last

Every app's settings screen opens with the same three-state Appearance control (Light / Dark / System), then the app's own sections, then a single "Send feedback" row as the last item. The order is identical in all five apps.

Sheet for a transient task

The feedback form is the only sheet. It has a grab handle, a title, a backdrop, a close button and Escape. Focus stays inside it while it is open and returns to the trigger on close.

Empty states say what to do next

An empty group keeps its heading and shows a sentence, never a blank area: "Nothing yet. 870 kcal left for the day." The heading stays so the layout does not jump when the first row arrives.

Counting reads as a sentence

Counts are words, not bare numbers: "3 open · 2 done", "4 notes", "1 task". A filtered list says so — "2 of 4 entries" — instead of pretending the list is smaller than it is.
